Daegu Day Trip
— a half-day food & walking route, Banwoldang to Seomun Market

The classic downtown Daegu walk: a 400-year-old herbal medicine alley, the Modern Culture Alley, and a huge traditional market — planned around one thing most guides skip: where to eat. Written by a restaurant that simmers soup in the herbal alley every day.

About 3 km on foot · 3–4 hours incl. lunch · Start: Banwoldang Station (Metro Lines 1 & 2)

Why this route

Search "things to do in Daegu" and the Modern Culture Alley walk always comes up — Cheongna Hill, Gyesan Cathedral and Yangnyeongsi strung into one old-town stroll. What most itineraries leave out is the important part: what to eat along the way. This guide flips that, and plans the walk around the food.

We also walk the official route in reverse: start at Banwoldang Station (the easiest place to reach), have lunch in the herbal alley, wander the old lanes, and finish with street food at Seomun Market.

Stop by stop

1

Banwoldang Station — start

The downtown junction of Daegu Metro Lines 1 and 2. Coming by KTX, transfer at Dongdaegu Station to Line 1 — about 15 minutes. Leave by the exit for "The Hyundai Daegu", walk north along the lane beside the department store for 3 minutes, and you will start to smell the medicinal herbs.

4-minute walk to the next stop

2

Yangnyeongsi herbal alley — lunch in a 400-year-old market

This herbal market opened in the 1650s and still trades today. The air literally smells different as you pass the herb shops; the free Daegu Yangnyeongsi Museum of Oriental Medicine mid-alley is worth 20–30 minutes.

Lunch is easiest right here in the alley. Cheongwoo Haejang simmers beef brisket and shank all day for a clear broth — the signatures are galbitang (beef short-rib soup) and spicy braised beef ribs, one of Daegu's famous "10 flavors". After days of spicy travel food, a clear beef soup is a welcome reset, and it works well for families. In summer there is Pyongyang-style cold noodle too. An English menu is available — just point to order.

5-minute walk to the next stop

3

Jin Alley — a cup of ssanghwa tea

Said to be the oldest lane in Daegu. Inside is Mido Dabang, a retro tearoom famous across Korea; the signature is ssanghwa herbal tea served with an egg yolk. Perfect timing right after lunch.

7-minute walk to the next stop

4

Gyesan Cathedral & the Yi Sang-hwa House

Daegu's first Gothic cathedral, completed in 1902, stands next to the preserved house of poet Yi Sang-hwa. The most photogenic stretch of the whole walk.

5-minute walk to the next stop

5

Cheongna Hill & the 3·1 Movement stairs

"The hill of green ivy." The 90 steps that students secretly climbed during the 1919 independence movement are still here, along with century-old missionary houses. This is where the official route starts — we save it for last.

12–15 minutes on foot to Seomun Market (or one stop by bus)

6

Seomun Market — finish with street food

The largest traditional market in southeastern Korea. Arriving already fed is actually ideal — everything here is snack-sized. Must-tries: nappjak-mandu (flat dumplings, born in Daegu), hand-cut noodles in Kalguksu Alley, seed hotteok pancakes, and the bibimbap stalls. On some evenings the night market opens with even more.

TIP — The route is mostly flat; the only stairs are at Cheongna Hill, so comfortable shoes are enough. In summer, use the air-conditioned medicine museum and Mido Dabang as cool-down stops.

FAQ

Can I do this as a day trip from Busan or Seoul?
Yes. Busan → Dongdaegu is about 50 minutes by KTX (Seoul is about 1 hour 40 minutes), then 15 minutes by subway to Banwoldang. The route itself takes 3–4 hours including lunch.
Do I need Korean?
No. The sights have English signs, and Cheongwoo Haejang has an English menu — showing your phone screen to the staff is enough to order.
Is it OK with kids or older parents?
Yes. It is mostly flat, and the clear (non-spicy) soups at lunch suit every generation.
