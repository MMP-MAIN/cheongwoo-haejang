What is ttaro gukbap, and why Daegu?

Ttaro gukbap (따로국밥) literally means "rice and soup, separately." It was born in Daegu in the years after the Korean War: a rich beef broth simmered from brisket and bone, seasoned with red pepper and garlic, with the rice served in its own bowl so it stays fluffy. Daegu's city government lists it among the Daegu 10-mi, the ten dishes that define the city's food — alongside jjim-galbi (braised ribs) and yukgaejang.

Our version is the Daegu spicy beef soup on the menu: brisket simmered from morning to evening, gently spicy rather than punishing, served with rice on the side the Daegu way. If you want to taste the one dish that is truly Daegu, this is it.

Not a fan of spice? The same broth, without chili

Everything here starts from one pot: beef brisket and shank simmered all day into a clear, deep stock. That means the non-spicy dishes are just as good — and they are what most families and older guests order.

How to get here from Banwoldang, The Hyundai or Seomun Market

We are in Yangnyeongsi, the herbal medicine alley that has traded ginseng and medicinal roots since the 1600s, right in the middle of downtown Daegu (Jung-gu).

  • From Banwoldang Station (Lines 1 & 2): take the exit toward The Hyundai Daegu department store, walk north along the side of the store for 3 minutes, pass the Yangnyeongsi Museum of Oriental Medicine, and turn left into the alley — about 5 minutes on foot.
  • From Seomun Market: 10–12 minutes on foot east along Dalgubeol-daero, or one stop on Line 2.
  • From the Modern Culture Alley (Gyesan Cathedral, Cheongna Hill): 5 minutes — we are the natural lunch stop on that walking route.
Parking — Yangnyeongsi West Gate public car park is a 1-minute walk. Map & directions
